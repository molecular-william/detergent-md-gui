It is the placement, and nothing after it: what it does not show
  • No solvent and no ions. The build leaves a dry system: water and salt are added by the first equilibration leg. That is the build's own artifact, not a reduction of it.
  • Not the box you will simulate. x and y come from the protein's extent plus the margin, z from the initial z or the membrane slab; the NPT leg changes it, so the density here is not the run's.
  • Not a verdict on the micelle. A placement is geometry, not dynamics: whether the belt closes and survives is decided by the run's own assessment, after CG MD.
  • Not the final system. The assessment leg removes the detergents that are not in the micelle, and the back-map turns the surviving state atomistic; neither is here.
  • Beads, not chemistry. A coarse-grained bead has no element, so the file writes a neutral stand-in (pdc_view states it) and colours each from its own Martini 3 polarity family. With a mapping the picture is that mapping's beads and bonds; with none, the build records one centre per molecule, writes no topology, and every bead is the family-not-stated colour.
Where this picture comes from, what it costs, and when there is none

Where it comes from. The build's own placement step, run on demand: pdc_build.select_protein, the coarse-grained protein, pdc_placement.place_detergents, pdc_box, pdc_build.stamp and pdc_view.system_pdb — the functions the stage calls, in the order it calls them. Nothing here is drawn by hand: a schematic would look like a prediction of a system these inputs do not produce, which is the one thing this card will not do.

When the build refuses, this shows the refusal and no picture. The template's own validator and the placement's clearance gate are the build's gates, and the card renders the builder's own sentence rather than inventing a message or drawing a system the build would refuse. One scope worth knowing: a refusal from the sphere is not a verdict on the protein — the sphere is the shape a build makes when the protein's file states no membrane, and a file that states one is placed as a band in the membrane plane instead.

With no protein there is no coarse-graining at all. The empty micelle is placed directly around the box the count and the radius define, so it is the cheapest case this card has, and there is no belt to size.

What it costs. The expensive half is the coarse-graining, and only that half depends on the protein and its chain ticks. Measured on this machine with 1AT9: martinize2 takes 6.7 s for one 231-residue chain and 13.6 s for all three (690 residues); the placement itself is 5–40 ms. The Cost row reports what this derive actually paid, and an empty micelle pays none of it. Once the coarse-grained protein exists, the count, the style, the seed and the box margins re-derive in about a tenth of a second.

What is kept. The frames are not: they are derived per request, because a run re-derives its own placement.pdb in its own stage. What is kept is the coarse-grained protein, in this workspace's own preview/cg/ directory (~50 KB per protein-and-selection), keyed by the protein's own bytes, the subunit ticks and the martinize2 that built it — so a change to any of those three derives it again, and a change to anything else does not. Nothing a run reads lives there, and a preview creates no run, no job and no manifest: it never appears in the runs list.

Why the card does not redraw on every keystroke. The picture is asked for: the reader presses Derive the preview. A change to the protein, the subunit ticks or the mapping marks the frame as no longer this form's and says so, rather than quietly drawing a picture that answers a different question. The count, the style, the seed and the box margins are re-derived as they change, because the cached coarse-grained protein makes those cheap.
