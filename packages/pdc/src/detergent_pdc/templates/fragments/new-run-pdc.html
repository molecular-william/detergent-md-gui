CG production

The first CG leg is named for what happens to it: its end state is judged and the detergents outside the micelle are removed. Losing more than a tenth of the requested count re-runs the leg with new velocities, up to three attempts. The extension continues the survivor from its checkpoint (gmx convert-tpr -extend), so the total CG sampling is both durations together — 1 µs by default — and that second assessment is the state that is back-mapped. Unrestrained: both are released.

The physics below is shared by both legs. The extension's own timestep applies only when the extension restarts the system; the continue path keeps the assessment tpr's dt.

What these numbers mean

These are the run-wide numbers: output cadence and cut-offs (the CG production legs), salt, seed, box and execution. Each stage's own numbers — including its neighbour list, temperature and couplings — are in its editor above; a leg's timestep, where blank, is that leg's reviewed profile dt (5 fs equilibration, 20 fs production) and a stated number converts every duration to steps at that number.

nstxtcout is the compressed-trajectory stride; newer profiles spell the same option nstxout-compressed. Full-precision nstxout, nstvout and nstfout stay off, as the profiles state.

The seed defaults to today's date (YYYYMMDD). Detergents are placed at random in the self-assembly style, so two builds are comparable only when both record the seed they placed with; clearing it stops the run rather than letting one be chosen for it.

The GPU list follows the CUDA ordinal, not nvidia-smi's index. The bus address stays in the run's record so the chosen device can be checked.
