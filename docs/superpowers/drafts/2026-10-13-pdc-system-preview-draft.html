DRAFT — the proposed system preview, mocked inside the real panel. Nothing is wired: no run is queued and no request is sent.

The pictures in this file are real output of the pipeline's own placement code, generated once for this mock by running pdc_placement, pdc_box and pdc_view.system_pdb — the same functions the build calls — against the protein the workspace actually holds (workspace/dashboard/proteins/1at9.pdb, bacteriorhodopsin, half-thickness 1.48 nm, chains A/B/C) and the DDM mapping the store actually holds. What is stubbed is the service: the panel does not fetch them, it swaps between five frames this file carries. The app derives one for whatever the form says, through the route named below.

What was not a run, and is therefore said here rather than hidden: three deviations were needed to obtain the frames, because the build cannot currently reach its own placement step for a protein. They are in the plan beside this draft; each is a live defect in the stage, not a choice the preview makes.

Where the picture comes from is the design call this mock makes. There are three honest answers and one dishonest one:

  • (a) run the whole builder into a scratch directory and render what it produced — faithful, and it costs a coarse-graining step plus a stage tree (measured: 19 MB of scratch);
  • (b) render the build's own placement step — protein and detergents in the box, no solvent — which is the same frame the run records as its own first snapshot, and is the last thing the reader's inputs decide. This is what the mock shows.
  • (c) render an illustrative schematic — the dishonest one: it would look like a prediction of a system these inputs do not produce. Not done, and the panel says it is not done.

The count default is 150 and the panel's own default protein is "No protein · empty micelle", so the mock opens on an empty micelle. Switch Initialisation style and the count and watch the picture answer; tick subunits under Protein; and try 1AT9 on the pre-assembled style — the build refuses that placement, and the panel shows the builder's own reason instead of a picture. The two columns' geometry is printed at the foot of this page and was measured in a browser, not read off the stylesheet.

It is a prediction — of the placement, and of nothing else. Detergents are placed with the run's own seed, and both generators are deterministic in it, so for these inputs this frame is the frame the run will build. Nothing after it is predicted: not the belt's final size, not the box, not whether a micelle survives.

What it does not show.

  • No solvent and no ions. The build leaves a dry system; water and salt are added in the first equilibration leg. Nothing in this picture is in water.
  • Not the box you will simulate. This is the box the build writes — x and y from the protein's own extent plus the margin, z at the initial z. The NPT leg changes it, so the density here is not the run's density.
  • Not a verdict on the micelle. A placement is geometry, not dynamics: whether the belt closes and survives is decided by the run's own assessment, after CG MD. This panel never decides it.
  • Not the final system. The assessment leg removes the detergents that are not in the micelle, and the back-map turns the surviving state atomistic; neither is here.
Where this picture comes from, what it costs, and when there is none

Where it comes from (the design call). The build's own placement step, run on demand — option (b) of the three honest answers. The alternative, "run the whole build" (a), costs the same coarse-graining step and writes a stage tree for a run that does not exist (measured: 19 MB); the one this panel will not do (c) is to draw a schematic, which would look like a prediction of a system these inputs do not produce. The placement is the part the reader can still change, and it is the part the run's own first snapshot shows.

When the build refuses, this shows the refusal and no picture. The placement has gates — the template's own validity, and the detergents' clearance from the protein — and the preview reports the builder's reason rather than drawing a system the build would refuse. Measured example: 1AT9 on the pre-assembled style puts a detergent 0.174 nm from the protein, under the 0.5 nm the builder requires, and no frame exists.

With no protein there is no coarse-graining at all. The empty micelle is placed directly around the box the count and the radius define, so it is the cheapest case the panel has and needs no CG step — and there is no belt to size, which is why the estimate is replaced by the detergent's own experimental aggregation number.

What it costs. Measured on this machine: the coarse-graining is martinize2 at 6.7 s for one 231-residue chain and 13.6 s for all three (690 residues), peaking at ~224-254 MB; the placement itself is 5-40 ms; an empty micelle needs no coarse-graining at all. Because the coarse-grained protein depends only on the protein and its chain ticks, it is cached outside every run (~50 KB per protein-and-selection), which is what makes a re-derive of the count, the style or the seed cheap.

What is kept. The frames are not: they are written to a scratch directory that no run reads, because a run re-derives its own snapshot in its own stage. One frame measured 195 KB at 150 detergents with the protein and 156 KB for an empty micelle; the cached coarse-grained protein is ~50 KB.

Why the panel does not redraw on every keystroke. The picture is asked for rather than streamed: the reader presses Derive the preview, and a change to the protein, the subunit ticks or the mapping marks the picture as no longer this form's until it is derived again. The count, the style, the seed and the box margins are re-derived as they are typed, because the cached CG protein makes those cheap. That is the same rule the estimate follows: what is on screen says which question it answers.

WORKFLOW

What this run will do

  • 1 · Prepare (optional)PDB2PQR for missing side chains and protonation, then energy minimisation in water. MemProtMD ships this as its own workflow; a structure that needs it is prepared once and reused.
  • 2 · Coarse-grainmartinize2, Martini 3, elastic network over Cα pairs within 1.0 nm at 1000 kJ/mol. This is what the preview above already did.
  • 3 · Build the micelleThe micelle is initialised pre-assembled with exactly the count you ask for, as CHARMM-GUI's Micelle Builder builds it.
  • 4 · SampleCG MD: minimisation, then equilibration, then the assessment leg. Solvation and ions arrive here, which is why the preview has none.
  • 5 · JudgeDetergents in the micelle are counted, the rest removed; losing more than 10% of the requested count re-runs the leg, up to three attempts.
  • 6 · Extend, then back-mapContinuation from the surviving checkpoint, then CG2AT2 with -o align.

Empty micelle: step 3 is the micelle on its own, and step 5's protein clause does not apply.

Memembed is skipped, and that is a saving rather than a gap: an OPM structure is already oriented in the membrane, which is the step it would do.
