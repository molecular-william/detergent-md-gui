DRAFT — the proposed system preview, mocked inside the real panel. Nothing is wired: no run is queued and no request is sent.

The pictures in this file are real output of the pipeline's own placement code, re-derived 2026-10-14 through the fixed stamp for the same inputs as before — pdc_placement.place_detergents, pdc_box, pdc_build.stamp and pdc_view.system_pdb, the same functions the build calls — against the protein the workspace actually holds (workspace/dashboard/proteins/1at9.pdb, bacteriorhodopsin, half-thickness 1.48 nm, chains A/B/C) and the DDM mapping the store actually holds. Each frame came back the same size as the one it replaces (198 961 / 284 604 / 156 136 / 156 141 / 62 626 bytes), with the same REMARK, CONECT and END lines and exactly the detergent HETATM records changed — 1 650 in each 150-detergent frame, 660 in the 60-detergent one — which is the per-molecule rotation about each detergent's own centre that the fix is. What is stubbed is the service: the panel does not fetch them, it swaps between five frames this file carries. The app derives one for whatever the form says, through the route named below.

The orientation defect, found by looking at these frames, and what the fix did to the picture. Every detergent used to be stamped head-inward. Measured off the frames this file carried until today: each molecule's head beads lay 0.317 nm inward of its own centre and its tails 0.845 nm outward, identically in all 660 molecules, while every lattice centre sat at exactly the placement radius (3.000 nm). The placement's direction is the way out of the micelle, so the fix ties the molecule's head-ward axis to it, and the same measurement now reads heads +0.317 nm outward, tails −0.845 nm, in 660 of 660 molecules. A micelle's heads face the water and its tails fill the core, which is what the frames here show; the pre-fix frames were the record of the defect and of its size, and that record — with both measurements — is kept in the plan rather than in this file.

What was not a run, and is therefore said here rather than hidden: the frames were derived in a scratch directory — the protein copied in, the mapping read, nothing written under workspace/ — and none of them needed a deviation: the build itself ran to exit 0 for all five. The three defects an earlier draft of this mock had to work around (the CG step reading a structure martinize2 never writes, a martinize2 command with no room for its own warning, and a subunit selection carrying a ligand martinize2 cannot type) are fixed in today's tree, which is why the frames now come from the build's own code path.

Two drawing choices, both measured in the browser rather than eyeballed. The beads are drawn at the radius their own size class has in the model (ngl-beads.js's table: R 0.264 / S 0.230 / T 0.191 nm), doubled because the preview is a reading view, and the bonds are thinner: the sticks are drawn at a quarter of the bead where the panel drew them at half — measured on the drawn buffers, the ball stayed 2.64 Å and the stick went from 1.32 Å to 0.66 Å for a tail bead. ball+stick draws the ball at radiusScale × aspectRatio × atomRadius and the stick at radiusScale × atomRadius, so the two knobs are independent and the ball did not shrink.

Where the picture comes from is the design call this mock makes. There are three honest answers and one dishonest one:

  • (a) run the whole builder into a scratch directory and render what it produced — faithful, and it costs a coarse-graining step plus a stage tree (measured: 19 MB of scratch);
  • (b) render the build's own placement step — protein and detergents in the box, no solvent — which is the same frame the run records as its own first snapshot, and is the last thing the reader's inputs decide. This is what the mock shows.
  • (c) render an illustrative schematic — the dishonest one: it would look like a prediction of a system these inputs do not produce. Not done, and the panel says it is not done.

The count default is 150 and the panel's own default protein is "No protein · empty micelle", so the mock opens on an empty micelle. Switch Initialisation style and the count and watch the picture answer; tick subunits under Protein and the frames show the self-assembled ones. One branch is a stand-in and says so where it appears: 1AT9 on the pre-assembled style shows the builder's refusal, and that sentence is measured on the sphere lattice — what a build makes when the protein's file states no membrane. 1AT9's file does state one (half-thickness 1.48 nm), so today's code places this belt as a band in the membrane plane and passes its gate (measured 0.5436 nm from the protein, valid); the mock carries no band frame, so the branch shows the refusal state with its sentence scoped to the sphere. The measurement is in the plan. The two columns' geometry is printed at the foot of this page and was measured in a browser, not read off the stylesheet.

It is a prediction — of the placement, and of nothing else. Detergents are placed with the run's own seed, and both generators are deterministic in it, so for these inputs this frame is the frame the run will build. Nothing after it is predicted: not the belt's final size, not the box, not whether a micelle survives.

Heads to the water, tails to the core — and beads drawn twice as large as the model's own radius, on bonds drawn at a quarter of a bead. The placement's direction is the way out of the micelle, away from the protein, and each detergent is stamped with its head along it: a micelle's heads face the water and its tails fill the core. (Fixed 2026-10-14: before that the stamp tied the molecule's tail-ward axis to the direction, so every frame drew a micelle inside out — the frames this file carries were re-derived through the fix, and the pre-fix measurement is in the banner.) The bead radius is a drawing choice, because a coarse-grained bead has no element and no size of its own: it is the model's radius for the class the file states (`ngl-beads.js`'s table), doubled for reading, and the model's own bonds are drawn with it — at `BEAD_STICK_FRACTION`, a quarter of the bead, since 2026-10-14 (measured in the browser: the ball stayed 2.64 Å and the stick went from 1.32 Å to 0.66 Å).

What it does not show.

  • No solvent and no ions. The build leaves a dry system; water and salt are added in the first equilibration leg. Nothing in this picture is in water.
  • No bead sizes in these files. The panel draws each bead at the radius its own size class has in the model (R 0.264 / S 0.230 / T 0.191 nm), but a run's viewer file writes the same stand-in element C for every bead and pdc_view has no class to state, so every bead here is drawn as the model's R. Nothing in this picture therefore says a head bead is smaller than a tail one, because the file does not; the per-class sizes appear where a file states them.
  • Not the box you will simulate. This is the box the build writes — x and y from the protein's own extent plus the margin, z at the initial z. The NPT leg changes it, so the density here is not the run's density.
  • Not a verdict on the micelle. A placement is geometry, not dynamics: whether the belt closes and survives is decided by the run's own assessment, after CG MD. This panel never decides it.
  • Not the final system. The assessment leg removes the detergents that are not in the micelle, and the back-map turns the surviving state atomistic; neither is here.
Where this picture comes from, what it costs, and when there is none

Where it comes from (the design call). The build's own placement step, run on demand — option (b) of the three honest answers. The alternative, "run the whole build" (a), costs the same coarse-graining step and writes a stage tree for a run that does not exist (measured: 19 MB); the one this panel will not do (c) is to draw a schematic, which would look like a prediction of a system these inputs do not produce. The placement is the part the reader can still change, and it is the part the run's own first snapshot shows.

When the build refuses, this shows the refusal and no picture. The placement has gates — the template's own validity, and the detergents' clearance from the protein — and the preview reports the builder's reason rather than drawing a system the build would refuse. The refusal this mock shows is the sphere lattice's, and it is scoped that way where it appears: a build whose protein file states no membrane places a sphere of the fixed 3.0 nm radius about the centroid, and 1AT9 on that path puts a detergent 0.174 nm from the protein — under the 0.5 nm gate, so no frame exists. 1AT9's own file does state a membrane, and the code now places a belt band in it: measured today on the build's own coarse-grained chain A, the band's radius is 3.6408 nm and the clearance is 0.5436 nm — valid. The mock carries no band frame, so that branch shows the refusal state rather than a picture; the honest sentence for the built panel is the band's. The earlier note's advice still holds: choosing fewer chains is not a way out of the sphere's refusal — the lattice gives chain A alone 0.053 nm, A+B 0.034 nm and the trimer 0.050 nm — and self-assembly's clean 0.50–0.56 nm is its sampler rejecting anything closer than the gate it is then judged by, not a better fit.

With no protein there is no coarse-graining at all. The empty micelle is placed directly around the box the count and the radius define, so it is the cheapest case the panel has and needs no CG step — and there is no belt to size, which is why the estimate is replaced by the detergent's own experimental aggregation number.

What it costs. Measured on this machine: the coarse-graining is martinize2 at 6.7 s for one 231-residue chain and 13.6 s for all three (690 residues), peaking at ~224-254 MB; the placement itself is 5-40 ms; an empty micelle needs no coarse-graining at all. Because the coarse-grained protein depends only on the protein and its chain ticks, it is cached outside every run (~50 KB per protein-and-selection), which is what makes a re-derive of the count, the style or the seed cheap.

What is kept. The frames are not: they are written to a scratch directory that no run reads, because a run re-derives its own snapshot in its own stage. One frame measured 195 KB at 150 detergents with the protein and 156 KB for an empty micelle; the cached coarse-grained protein is ~50 KB.

Why the panel does not redraw on every keystroke. The picture is asked for rather than streamed: the reader presses Derive the preview, and a change to the protein, the subunit ticks or the mapping marks the picture as no longer this form's until it is derived again. The count, the style, the seed and the box margins are re-derived as they are typed, because the cached CG protein makes those cheap. That is the same rule the estimate follows: what is on screen says which question it answers.

WORKFLOW

What this run will do

  • 1 · Prepare (optional)PDB2PQR for missing side chains and protonation, then energy minimisation in water. MemProtMD ships this as its own workflow; a structure that needs it is prepared once and reused.
  • 2 · Coarse-grainmartinize2, Martini 3, elastic network over Cα pairs within 1.0 nm at 1000 kJ/mol. This is what the preview above already did.
  • 3 · Build the micelleThe micelle is initialised pre-assembled with exactly the count you ask for, as CHARMM-GUI's Micelle Builder builds it.
  • 4 · SampleCG MD: minimisation, then equilibration, then the assessment leg. Solvation and ions arrive here, which is why the preview has none.
  • 5 · JudgeDetergents in the micelle are counted, the rest removed; losing more than 10% of the requested count re-runs the leg, up to three attempts.
  • 6 · Extend, then back-mapContinuation from the surviving checkpoint, then CG2AT2 with -o align.

Empty micelle: step 3 is the micelle on its own, and step 5's protein clause does not apply.

Memembed is skipped, and that is a saving rather than a gap: an OPM structure is already oriented in the membrane, which is the step it would do.
